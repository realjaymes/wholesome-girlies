You now own the whole journey. Four full guides, one for each stage, plus this one.

So what is this one for?

The four guides go deep inside each stage. This guide is the map that holds them together. It does the one thing a single-stage guide cannot do. It walks you across the joins, from one stage to the next, where most women feel the most lost.

Think about it. The scariest moments in motherhood are rarely the middle of a stage. They are the handoffs. The morning the test is positive and you have no idea what to do first. The day you carry a newborn through your own front door and the hospital is behind you. The week your baby stops being a newborn and becomes a small person with opinions. Nobody hands you a map for those crossings. This is that map.

You do not read this cover to cover today. You read the part you are standing in, and you glance at the part that is coming, so it stops being a surprise.

Here is how everything fits.

The whole journey at a glance

Four stages. Four guides. One community, and a set of tools on Wholesome Girlies, ready whenever you need them. Everything named here is a link you can open.

Stage one, trying. The Trying-to-Conceive Blueprint. Your cycle, your body, your partner's part in it, your numbers, and when trying has gone on long enough to see a doctor. You hold steady while you wait.

Stage two, pregnancy. The First Pregnancy Plan. Week by week, what is normal and what is not, what you can eat, what your scans and tests mean, and getting ready for birth and the first days.

Stage three, the weeks after birth. The 6-Week Postpartum Reset. The mother, not just the baby. Your body healing, your marriage shifting, and your mind, with a clear guide to when a hard day is something more.

Stage four, the baby years. The First Baby Playbook. Is this normal, sleep, feeding and solids, milestones and growth, and knowing what is wrong and how to keep your baby safe.

You can enter at any stage. Most women do. If you are already pregnant, start in stage two and keep stage one for another time. Nothing is wasted. It is all yours, for good, gathered on your Motherhood Journey home. It shows where you stopped reading, and in the Girlies app you open it with the My program tab.

Two things sit under all four stages, for the whole journey.

Your community. One warm, moderated space, wherever you are in the world. Join the community here. There is a private assistant inside for your questions at any hour, and it always points you to a doctor for anything clinical.

Your tools. Trackers, checklists, planners, and check-ins, one set per stage, all on Wholesome Girlies. Browse all your tools here. Your guides point you to them at the right moment. You never have to hunt.

The one thread that runs through everything

Before the stages, learn the thread. It is the same three questions at every single stage, from the first month of trying to the toddler years. When you have these three, you are never fully lost, even on a bad day.

What is normal. Most of what worries you is common and passes. Knowing the wide, normal range is what stops a normal thing from stealing your peace.

What is coming next. Fear lives in surprise. When you can see the next bend in the road before you reach it, it stops being frightening and becomes just the next thing.

When to worry. This is the one that matters most. Each stage has a short, clear list of signs that mean stop reading and get real care. You are not expected to diagnose yourself. You are only expected to know the handful of signs that mean pick up the phone. Every guide gives you that list for its stage. When in doubt, you always call. A wasted trip to the clinic is a small price. Waiting when you should not is not.

Hold those three. Everything else in every guide hangs off them.

The crossings

This is the heart of this guide. Five crossings, in order. Read the one you are about to make.

Before trying, into trying

You have decided. Now the waiting starts, and waiting is its own kind of work.

What is normal. For a healthy couple under 35, it can take up to a year of regular trying. Under a year is not failure. It is the normal spread. Some months your timing will be off, some months nothing lines up, and that is ordinary.

What is coming next. You will learn your own cycle, find your fertile window, and get both your bodies as ready as they reasonably can be. Your partner is half of this, so his part starts now, not later. The Trying-to-Conceive Blueprint walks all of it.

When to worry, or rather, when to get help. See a doctor after about a year of trying if you are under 35, about six months if you are 35 or over, or sooner if your periods are irregular or you have a known condition. Getting help early is wisdom, not defeat. If the waiting is weighing heavily on your mind, that counts too, and you can reach MANI in Nigeria on 0809 111 6264.

Your home base for this stage is your Conception space in the community, kept separate on purpose from bump-and-baby talk, because the last thing you need while trying is a feed full of newborns.

Trying, into pregnant

The test is positive. Joy and panic arrive together. Breathe. Here is the crossing.

The first three things. Book your first antenatal appointment. Start a daily folic acid if you are not already, because it protects your baby's development in these early weeks. And be gentle with the fear. Early pregnancy is exciting and nerve-racking at once, and that mix is normal.

What is coming next. Your first weeks are mostly invisible from the outside and very busy on the inside. Tiredness and nausea are common and usually settle. The First Pregnancy Plan takes you from here, week by week, and tells you what each scan and test is actually for so the hospital stops feeling like a place of riddles.

When to worry. Two rules carry the whole of pregnancy, so learn them the day you cross over. Any bleeding, and any severe or one-sided pain, means contact your doctor or clinic straight away, not tomorrow. These are not things to wait out or read about first. When you are unsure, you call. That instinct will serve you for nine months.

You now move from the Conception space into the shared community, where women who are carrying and raising are a step ahead of you.

Pregnant, into birth and the first days home

Late pregnancy turns into birth, and birth turns, faster than anyone warns you, into carrying a newborn through your own front door with no midwife attached.

What is coming next. Your Pregnancy Plan gets you ready for birth itself, your bag, your plan, your choices. Then the ground shifts. At home, the attention that was on your bump for nine months moves entirely to the baby, and you, the person who just gave birth, are quietly expected to get on with it. You will not just get on with it, and you are not meant to.

The handoff itself. This is the exact join where The 6-Week Postpartum Reset takes over. Before you are discharged, that guide becomes your next home base. Your body is healing from something enormous. Feeding is being learned by two people at once. Sleep is broken. All of that is the normal shape of the first weeks, and the Reset walks you through every part of it, including your marriage and your mind, not only the baby.

When to worry. The first weeks after birth have their own short red-flag list, and it is the most important one in the whole journey. Heavy bleeding that soaks a pad in an hour, a high fever, a hot painful area in your breast or your wound, chest pain or trouble breathing, or thoughts that frighten you, any of these means get care straight away. The full list lives in your Reset, in the When to See a Doctor guide. Read it before you give birth, not after.

The first weeks, into the baby months

Around six weeks, something quietly turns. The rawest part is behind you. Your baby stops being a brand-new newborn and starts becoming a small person.

What is coming next. The intense healing weeks ease. Feeding settles into a rhythm. Your baby starts to smile, to notice, to have a personality. The questions change too. They stop being about your stitches and start being about their sleep, their feeding, are they growing right, is this milestone late. That is the moment The First Baby Playbook takes over from the Reset.

When to worry. Your own six-week check matters, so keep it, and take your questions written down. For your baby, learn the recognise-and-route signs in the Playbook: the signs of a baby who is unwell, and what a real feeding or breathing problem looks like versus normal fuss. You are not becoming a nurse. You are learning the short list that means call the clinic.

A note on sleep, because it causes more worry and more bad advice than almost anything. Follow the safe-sleep guidance in your Playbook exactly, every sleep, day and night. And ignore anyone, online or in your family, who promises a schedule that will have a small baby sleeping all night. Safe and settled is the goal, not silent.

The baby years, and onward

The Playbook carries you through feeding and solids, milestones, growth and behaviour, and keeping a mobile, curious baby safe. This is the longest and gentlest stretch, and by now you have the thread. What is normal, what is next, when to worry. You know how to use it.

You own every stage already. So when the next one arrives, there is nothing to buy and nothing to find. It is already yours, already waiting.

How to actually use all of this

Do not try to hold four guides in your head. Do this instead.

Live in one guide at a time. Open the guide for the stage you are in. Read only what this week needs. The rest waits.

Glance at the next crossing. When you feel a stage ending, come back to this map on your Motherhood Journey home and read the crossing that is coming, so it arrives as the next thing, not a shock.

Keep the tools close. When a guide points you to a tracker, a checklist, or a check-in, open it on Wholesome Girlies and use it there. They are built to be used, not just read. All your tools are here.

Use the community as your night light. The hard moments rarely happen in clinic hours. Someone in the room is usually awake, wherever you are in the world. The assistant is there any hour, and it always sends anything clinical to a real doctor.

Book private help when you want more. A one-to-one with a licensed practitioner is there as an add-on whenever a question needs personal care. Your own doctor is always your first call for anything clinical or urgent.

Your safety net, across the whole journey

Every stage in this bundle has its own When to Worry list, inside its own guide. That is on purpose. The signs that matter while trying are not the signs that matter after birth.

You do not need to memorise all of them at once. You only need to know where each list is, and to trust the instinct this guide has been building in you the whole way through. When something feels wrong, you do not wait to be sure. You contact your doctor or your clinic.

For your mind, at any stage, trying, carrying, or raising, if a heaviness will not lift or thoughts are frightening you, that is a reason to reach out, not to push through alone. In Nigeria you can call MANI on 0809 111 6264.

You have the whole map now. You will not walk it perfectly, because nobody does. But you will not walk it lost. We are with you the entire way.
