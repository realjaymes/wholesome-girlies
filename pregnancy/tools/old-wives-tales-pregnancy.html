Old Wives' Tales: True or False? Pregnancy Edition

Ten things Nigerian families tell a pregnant woman, from bump shape to cold water. Tap True, False or It depends, then see the answer and the source. Educational, not medical advice.

Your answers, with the sources

    Educational, not medical advice. These are common beliefs checked against the sources listed below. They are not yet reviewed by a clinician, and they cannot tell you anything about your own pregnancy. The game runs on your phone. Nothing you pick is saved or sent anywhere.

    When to see a doctor or midwife

    Speak to your midwife or doctor before you take any herbal mixture, medicine or supplement in pregnancy. Go to your clinic or hospital the same day if you have bleeding, strong pain, a baby moving less than usual, or a feeling that something is wrong. Do not use an old wives' tale to decide whether something is wrong.

    Written by Cynthia Obinatu, community and editorial lead. Sourced, not yet reviewed by a clinician.

    How the Old Wives' Tales game works

    You will see ten things that families, aunties and neighbours tell pregnant women in Nigeria. Pick True, False or It depends. The answer and the source show straight away, so you learn something on every screen.

    1. Read one claim at a time.
    2. Tap True, False or It depends.
    3. Read the answer and open the source.
    4. See your score and send it to a friend.

    What True, False and It depends mean here

    True means the claim holds up in the sources. False means the sources do not support it. It depends means the claim is partly right, or right for one situation and wrong for another.

    Many of these beliefs come from people who love you and wanted to protect the baby in times when scans and clinics were far away. We explain each one and we never laugh at it. Faith and cultural practices, such as keeping a pin on your clothes or praying over a pregnancy, are not claims about the body, so the game does not rule on them. Pick the ones that comfort you, and keep your antenatal visits too.

    For more on the foods aunties have opinions about, read Tiger nut, bitter kola, cloves. To check a food on your plate, use the Safe Foods Checklist.

    Common questions

    Is this medical advice?
    No. The game is educational, and every answer is checked against the sources listed on the page. It is not yet reviewed by a clinician. Ask your midwife or doctor about your own pregnancy.
    Why do some answers say It depends?
    Some beliefs are partly right. Pawpaw is a good example, because unripe fruit is the concern and ripe fruit is a different case.
    Does False mean my family is wrong to care?
    No. Many of these tales come from care. The answer only checks the claim against the evidence.
    Is my score saved?
    No. Your picks stay on your phone. If you share your result, the link and the picture show your result only, never your picks.
