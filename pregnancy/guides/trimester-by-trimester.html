Guide

Pregnancy, trimester by trimester

Last updated: September 2026

Written by Cynthia Obinatu, community and editorial lead · Checked against published guidance from National Health Service, PubMed Central and World Health Organization. Not yet reviewed by a clinician, so read it with our medical policy.

Pregnancy is counted in three stages, and they genuinely feel different from one another rather than being an arbitrary way of dividing up nine months. Here is what tends to happen in each one, which appointments and scans belong where, and the parts that matter a great deal more here than the international guides written for somewhere else tend to suggest.

A map, not a description of yours. Two pregnancies can look nothing alike and both be fine. Your antenatal clinic and your scans are the real guide. This page has not been reviewed by a clinician. Anything in the warning list at the bottom overrides everything above it.

First trimester: weeks 1 to 12

The major organs form in these weeks, which is why they matter more than they feel like they do. Many women are exhausted and nauseated. Many feel almost nothing, and that is equally normal and not a sign that anything is wrong.

Three things belong in this window. Take 400 micrograms of folic acid daily through week 12 if you are not already. Register for antenatal care early rather than waiting to show. And expect a first scan somewhere around weeks 8 to 12, which dates the pregnancy more accurately than your own counting can.

Registering early is the one people delay, often to be sure the pregnancy is settled first. The trouble is that the checks done at registration, blood pressure, blood group, genotype, anaemia, are most useful when there is time to act on them.

Malaria, which the international guides underweight

This is where a guide written for the United Kingdom stops being enough. The World Health Organization lists pregnant women among those at higher risk of severe malaria infection, and notes that malaria in pregnancy can cause premature delivery or a baby with low birth weight. Nigeria accounts for the largest share of malaria deaths in the African region.

Which is why antenatal care here includes intermittent preventive treatment, the Fansidar doses given at your visits, alongside a treated net. Take them. Researchers asking women in Bayelsa State what shaped that decision found a clarity worth borrowing.

Second trimester: weeks 13 to 27

Usually the easiest stretch. Sickness tends to ease, energy comes back, and you start to show. The anomaly scan falls somewhere around weeks 18 to 22 and looks in detail at how the baby is developing. First movements arrive in this window for most women, earlier for second pregnancies.

Iron is checked again around week 28 in most systems, and anaemia is common in pregnancy across Nigeria, so this is the stretch to take iron seriously rather than the stretch to coast. Keep tea away from meals you are eating for iron, because it reduces how much you absorb.

It is also the practical window. Plan the birth, sort the money, have the conversations, while you still have the energy to do it.

Third trimester: weeks 28 to birth

The baby grows fast and you feel all of it. Backache, heartburn, breathlessness and broken sleep are the standard complaints, and none of them means something is wrong.

One thing in this trimester is not a comfort issue. Learn your baby's pattern of movement, and report any change in it the same day, not at the next appointment. Reduced movement is the sign clinicians most want to hear about early, and it is the one women most often talk themselves out of raising.

By about week 36, have the bag packed, know the route and the plan for getting there at 2am, and know the signs of labour and when to go in. Decide in advance who takes you, because working that out during contractions is how avoidable delays happen.

How many antenatal visits, and why they are not optional

The World Health Organization recommends a minimum of eight antenatal contacts through a pregnancy, up from the four that many services were built around. The visits are not a formality. They are where blood pressure is caught, anaemia is caught, position is checked and malaria prevention is given, and every one of those is something that is cheap to fix early and expensive to fix late.

When to see a doctor

Bleeding. Severe or persistent pain. Fever. Vomiting so severe you cannot keep fluids down. A bad headache with blurred vision or sudden swelling of the face and hands. Fluid leaking. Any change in your baby's movements after about 28 weeks. If you are unsure, call. Nobody minds a call that turns out to be nothing.

Common questions

What are the three trimesters of pregnancy?
The first is weeks 1 to 12, the second is weeks 13 to 27, and the third is week 28 until birth. They are counted from the first day of your last period, not from conception.
When should I have my first antenatal appointment?
As early as you can, ideally in the first trimester. The checks done at registration, blood pressure, blood group, genotype and anaemia, are most useful when there is time to act on them.
How many antenatal visits should I have?
The World Health Organization recommends a minimum of eight contacts across the pregnancy. Each one catches something that is cheap to fix early and expensive to fix late.
Which scans will I have?
Usually a dating scan around weeks 8 to 12 and a detailed anomaly scan around weeks 18 to 22. Your clinic may add others depending on your circumstances.
Is malaria prevention safe in pregnancy?
Yes, and it is part of routine antenatal care here for good reason. The World Health Organization lists pregnant women at higher risk of severe malaria, which can cause premature delivery or low birth weight. Take the doses your clinic gives you and sleep under a treated net.
When do you feel the baby move?
Most women feel first movements in the second trimester, often between weeks 18 and 24, and earlier in a second pregnancy. From about 28 weeks, learn your baby's pattern and report any change the same day.
When should I pack my hospital bag?
By about week 36. Also decide who is taking you and by what route, because working that out during contractions is how avoidable delays happen.
