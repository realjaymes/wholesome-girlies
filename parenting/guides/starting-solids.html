Starting solids: first foods, made simple

Last updated: September 2026

Written by Cynthia Obinatu, community and editorial lead · Checked against published guidance from Maternal & Child Nutrition, via PubMed Central, National Health Service, United Nations Children's Fund and World Health Organization. Not yet reviewed by a clinician, so read it with our medical policy.

Solids start at around six months, and the useful part of that sentence is the word around. Readiness is a set of three specific things your baby can do, not a date on a calendar, and there are four common behaviours that look like readiness and are not. Getting that distinction right saves most of the worry attached to this milestone.

Ask your clinic if your baby was premature. Timing is judged differently for a premature baby or one with a health condition, and that is a conversation rather than a rule. This guide has not been reviewed by a clinician.

The three signs that actually mean ready

The National Health Service names three, and the point is that they have to appear together, from around six months.

  • They can stay sitting and hold their head steady, because swallowing safely needs an upright position.
  • They can co-ordinate eyes, hands and mouth well enough to look at food, pick it up and get it in themselves.
  • They can swallow food rather than pushing it back out with their tongue.

The four signs that fool almost everybody

These get read as hunger and they are just babies being babies. The National Health Service lists them plainly: chewing their fists, waking more in the night, and wanting extra milk feeds.

And the belief underneath all three, which is the one worth naming. Starting solids will not make your baby sleep through the night. There is no evidence for it, it is the single most repeated reason for starting early, and a little extra milk usually settles the thing that solids were being asked to fix.

Milk is still the meal

For a good while yet, solids are practice rather than nutrition. Breast milk or formula stays the main source, and the first weeks of solids are about tastes, textures and learning to move food around the mouth.

The World Health Organization recommends exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months and continued breastfeeding alongside food up to two years or beyond, and estimates that nearly 400,000 deaths of children under five could be prevented each year if that were universal. Starting solids is an addition, not a handover.

Good first foods, from the market you already use

You do not need a baby aisle and you do not need imported jars. Start with one food at a time, soft, and unseasoned.

  • Mashed fruit: ripe banana, pawpaw, avocado.
  • Soft-cooked starches: sweet potato, yam, plantain, soft rice.
  • Well-cooked pap or ogi, thick enough to sit on a spoon rather than run off it.
  • Mashed beans, and soft-cooked vegetables such as carrot or ugu blended in.
  • Sips of water in an open cup with meals, from around six months.

Move on from smooth purée to mashed, lumpy and finger foods as soon as your baby can manage them. Staying on smooth too long is a more common problem than starting lumps too early, and finger foods are how a baby learns to bite, chew and swallow pieces rather than only to swallow.

One habit carries most of this, and it lands on whoever cooks. Researchers in Kaduna State asked families how the work divides, and the answer was consistent.

So the practical version of everything below is one movement at the stove: take the baby's portion out before the seasoning goes in. If somebody else is cooking, that instruction has to reach them, because it is the step that turns family food into baby food.

What to skip, and the reasons

  • Salt, including stock cubes, seasoning cubes and gravy. This is the one most Nigerian kitchens trip on, because a cube goes into everything by habit. Babies' kidneys cannot handle salt. Take a portion out before you season the pot.
  • Sugar, which causes tooth decay and trains a preference you will be arguing with for years.
  • Honey before one year, without exception.
  • Whole nuts and hard chunks, which are choking hazards. Ground or smooth nut butters are a different thing and are fine.
  • Unpasteurised milk and cheese, and cow's milk as a main drink before one year. Cow's milk in cooking or mixed into food from around six months is fine.

Allergy foods go in earlier than people expect

The advice here reversed some years ago and the old version is still repeated, so it is worth stating clearly. The National Health Service says foods containing allergens, such as peanut, hen's egg, gluten and fish, can be introduced from around six months, one at a time and in small amounts so a reaction can be spotted.

Delaying them does not protect your baby. Introduce one, wait a few days, watch, then introduce the next. Offer it earlier in the day rather than at night, so you are awake if something happens.

When to see a doctor

Swelling of the lips, tongue or face, a spreading rash or hives, vomiting straight after a new food, or any difficulty breathing. These are allergic reactions and they need care now, not in the morning. Also seek advice if your baby chokes rather than gags, refuses all food over several days, or is losing weight.

Common questions

When can a baby start solid food?
At around six months, when three signs appear together: they can sit and hold their head steady, co-ordinate eyes, hands and mouth to get food in themselves, and swallow rather than push food back out.
Will starting solids help my baby sleep through the night?
No. There is no evidence for it, and it is the most common reason people start early. Chewing fists, waking more and wanting extra milk are normal behaviours rather than signs of readiness. A little more milk usually settles it.
What are good first foods in Nigeria?
Mashed ripe banana, pawpaw or avocado, soft-cooked sweet potato, yam or plantain, well-cooked pap or ogi, mashed beans, and soft-cooked vegetables. No special baby products are needed.
What foods should babies avoid?
Added salt, including seasoning and stock cubes, added sugar, honey before one year, whole nuts and hard chunks, unpasteurised milk and cheese, and cow's milk as a main drink before one year.
Can I give my baby food from the family pot?
Yes, if you take their portion out before you add salt or seasoning cubes. That single habit is the main thing standing between family food and baby food.
When should I introduce peanut, egg and fish?
From around six months, one at a time and in small amounts, so a reaction can be spotted. Delaying them does not protect your baby, and the older advice to wait has been reversed.
How much should a baby eat when starting solids?
Very little at first, and milk remains the main source of nutrition. Babies do not need three meals a day when they start. The point in the early weeks is learning tastes and textures, not calories.
