Milestone Guess: When Do Babies Roll, Sit and Walk?

Guess the age for eight baby milestones, from the first smile to the first words. After each guess you see the usual range and where it comes from. Every baby moves at their own pace.

Your guesses, with the ranges

    Educational, not medical advice. Milestone ages are usual ranges, and they cannot tell you anything about your own baby. Babies develop at very different rates, and reaching something later is often completely normal. The game runs on your phone. Nothing you pick is saved or sent anywhere.

    When to speak to a doctor

    Mention it at a check-up if your baby is not meeting several milestones for their age, seems to lose a skill they had, does not respond to sounds or faces, or if your instinct says something is different. Early support helps, and asking is never an overreaction. A late milestone is not a failure, and a score in this game says nothing about your baby.

    Written by Cynthia Obinatu, community and editorial lead. Sourced, not yet reviewed by a clinician.

    How Milestone Guess works

    You will see eight milestones that many parents watch for. Guess the age by tapping one of four choices. You score 2 points for the usual age, 1 point for a near guess and 0 for a guess far off. The usual age and its source show straight away.

    1. Read one milestone at a time.
    2. Tap the age you think is usual.
    3. Read the range and open the source.
    4. See your score and send it to a friend.

    Where the ages come from

    The "by" ages come from the CDC's "Learn the Signs. Act Early." milestone lists, which show what most children (75 percent or more) can do by that age. The movement ages for sitting, crawling and walking come from the WHO Motor Development Study, which followed children from Ghana, India, Norway, Oman and the USA, and shows a wide window for each milestone. These are the same sources as the Baby Milestone Tracker.

    A range is not a deadline. The CDC says a milestone is something most children can do by an age, so some children reach it earlier and some later. Use the game to learn the ranges, and use your clinic visits to talk about your own baby.

    To see what many babies do at each age, open the Baby Milestone Tracker. For first foods after 6 months, read Starting solids.

    Common questions

    Is a late milestone a problem?
    Not by itself. Babies develop at very different rates, and the ranges are wide. Mention it at a check-up if your baby is not meeting several milestones, loses a skill, or you feel something is different.
    Why are some ages a range and others a "by" age?
    The CDC lists the age by which most children have reached a milestone. WHO gives a window from the earliest to the latest ages in a healthy group of children. The page says which one each answer uses.
    My baby never crawled. Is that a worry?
    In the WHO study, about 4 in every 100 babies never crawled on hands and knees. Some babies move around another way. Mention it at a check-up if you want to ask.
    Is my score saved?
    No. Your picks stay on your phone. If you share your result, the link and the picture show your result only, never your picks.
