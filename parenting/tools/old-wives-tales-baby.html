Old Wives' Tales: True or False? Baby Edition

Ten things families say about newborns and babies, from a coin on the navel to teething fever. Tap True, False or It depends, then see the answer, the source and what to do instead.

Your answers, with the sources

    Educational, not medical advice. These are common beliefs checked against the sources listed below. They are not yet reviewed by a clinician, and they cannot tell you anything about your own baby. The game runs on your phone. Nothing you pick is saved or sent anywhere.

    Get a baby checked the same day

    Take your baby to a clinic or hospital straight away if they are under 3 months old with a temperature of 38C or higher, are hard to wake, floppy, not feeding or passing fewer wet nappies, are breathing fast or with effort, have a fit, or have yellow skin or eyes (NHS: fever in children; NHS: jaundice in newborns). Ask your clinic before you put any oil, rub, drops or herbal mixture on or in a baby.

    Written by Cynthia Obinatu, community and editorial lead. Sourced, not yet reviewed by a clinician.

    How the Old Wives' Tales game works

    You will see ten things that families, aunties and neighbours say about newborns and babies. Pick True, False or It depends. The answer and the source show straight away. Where a belief is unsafe, the answer says why and what to do instead.

    1. Read one claim at a time.
    2. Tap True, False or It depends.
    3. Read the answer, the source and what to do instead.
    4. See your score and send it to a friend.

    What True, False and It depends mean here

    True means the claim holds up in the sources. False means the sources do not support it. It depends means the claim is right for one age or situation and wrong for another.

    Most of these practices come from love, and they were passed down by people who raised healthy children without clinics nearby. We explain each one and we never laugh at it. Shaving the head for a naming ceremony or a religious rite is a tradition families keep, and the game only checks the claim about hair thickness.

    To see what many babies do at each age, use the Baby Milestone Tracker. For first foods after 6 months, read Starting solids.

    Common questions

    Is this medical advice?
    No. The game is educational, and every answer is checked against the sources listed on the page. It is not yet reviewed by a clinician. Ask your clinic or paediatrician about your own baby.
    Why do some answers say It depends?
    Some beliefs are right at one age and wrong at another. Water for a baby is a good example, because it depends on whether the baby is under 6 months.
    Does False mean my family was wrong?
    No. These practices come from care, and many babies did well. The answer checks the claim against current evidence and says what to do instead.
    Is my score saved?
    No. Your picks stay on your phone. If you share your result, the link and the picture show your result only, never your picks.
