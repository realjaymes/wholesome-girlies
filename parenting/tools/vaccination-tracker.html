    When to speak to a doctor or nurse

    Ask your clinic if you have missed a vaccine, are unsure of the schedule where you live, or your baby was unwell around a due date. It is almost always fine to catch up, and they will guide you.

    The Nigerian childhood immunisation schedule

    This is the routine schedule Nigeria reports to the World Health Organization, which is the same one your health centre works from. The vaccines are free at government primary health centres. Nine visits carry a child from birth to nine years, and seven of them happen before the first birthday.

    6 weeksPentavalent 1, PCV 1, inactivated polio (IPV) 1, oral polio 1, rotavirus 132

    Pentavalent is one injection carrying five vaccines: diphtheria, tetanus, whooping cough, hepatitis B and Haemophilus influenzae type b. PCV protects against the pneumococcus, the commonest cause of bacterial pneumonia in small children. Counting the combinations separately is why the same schedule is sometimes described as twelve vaccines and sometimes as nine visits. It is the same schedule.

    Is this the latest immunisation schedule, and what about 2026?

    Yes, this is the current one. It is the schedule Nigeria reports to the World Health Organization, and the most recent record is the 2025 one, which is what your clinic is running now. There is no separate 2026 schedule waiting to be published. Searching for the year is a reasonable instinct, because charts on clinic walls do go out of date, but the schedule changes when a vaccine is added, not when a calendar turns.

    The last two changes were rubella joining the measles vaccine and HPV entering routine immunisation for girls, both covered below. If a third arrives, the table above changes and the date on this page changes with it.

    What are the current immunisation programmes in Nigeria?

    There are three, and people often mean different ones by the same phrase.

    Routine immunisation is the nine visits in the table above, given free at government primary health centres from birth through to the HPV dose for girls at nine. This is the one most people mean.

    Maternal immunisation runs alongside antenatal care. Pregnant women are offered tetanus-diphtheria doses across the pregnancy and into later years, which protects the mother and protects the newborn against tetanus in the first weeks. Vitamin A supplementation for children at 6 and 12 months sits in the same routine.

    Campaigns are the third. Nigeria runs periodic mass rounds outside the routine schedule, and World Health Organization records show polio and measles-rubella rounds run in 2024 and 2025. A campaign dose is an extra layer, not a replacement. If your child gets a polio dose at a campaign, they still need the routine ones on the card.

    How many injections will a 2 month old baby get?

    Nigeria's schedule is counted in weeks for the first few months, not months, so a baby around two months old is due the 6-week visit or the 10-week one depending on the exact date. The 6-week visit is three injections and two doses by mouth. The 10-week visit is two injections and two by mouth.

    Three injections in one sitting sounds like a lot, and most mothers find that visit the hardest one. Nurses usually give them in the thighs, one after the other, and it is over in under a minute. Feeding immediately afterwards settles most babies faster than anything else.

    What are the new vaccines on the schedule?

    Two things changed recently, and they are the reason an older chart on a clinic wall may not match what your baby is offered.

    Rubella joined the measles vaccine. Where the 9-month and 15-month visits used to give measles alone, they now give measles-rubella, which covers both in one injection. Rubella matters less for the child than for who the child meets later, because rubella caught in early pregnancy can seriously damage a developing baby.

    HPV entered routine immunisation for girls at nine years old. It is the vaccine against the human papillomavirus that causes almost all cervical cancer, and it works before exposure rather than after, which is why it is given years ahead of when anyone expects it to matter.

    What happens if you miss a visit

    You catch up, and you do not start again. This is the single most useful thing to know, because the fear of having ruined it keeps people away. A child who is behind is still offered the remaining doses on a catch-up plan, and the protection still builds. Take the card to the clinic and ask, even if the gap is a year.

    Missing a date is ordinary here. Fuel, a sick week, a move, a clinic out of stock. None of that makes your child unvaccinated. It makes them due.

    Keep the clinic card, and keep a copy

    The paper card the clinic gives you is the record that counts. It is asked for at school registration, it is asked for at travel, and it is the only proof of what has already been given. Cards get wet, get lost in a move, and fade. Photograph both sides after every visit and keep it in your phone.

    Yellow fever has its own document. The yellow card, the International Certificate of Vaccination, is a separate booklet from the child's immunisation card and is what border officials ask for. If you expect to travel, ask the clinic for it at the 9-month visit rather than chasing it years later.

    How this tracker works

    The tracker above holds the nine visits. Enter a date of birth and each one shows the date it falls due, so you are working from a date rather than counting weeks in your head. Ticking a visit saves on this device, and nothing is sent anywhere.

    It is a reminder, not a record. Schedules are revised, stock runs short, a state may run a campaign that adds something, and a doctor may vary the plan for a particular child. Your clinic card is the truth. If the two disagree, the card wins and this page is wrong.

    What your dates mean, and what they do not

    Each date is the day a visit becomes due, not a deadline. Clinics run immunisation on set days of the week, so the realistic plan is the next immunisation day at your centre on or after the date shown. Arriving a few days later changes nothing.

    The early dates are counted in weeks from birth and the later ones in months, which is why 14 weeks and 6 months look closer together on the calendar than the labels suggest. A baby born early is scheduled from the actual birth date, not the due date, so do not adjust these yourself. Ask the clinic if you are unsure.

    The 6-month visit tends to land in the same few weeks as first foods, so it is worth reading our guide to starting solids around the same time. If you are still in the early weeks, omugwo covers the support side of that period.

    Common questions

    What is the latest immunisation schedule in Nigeria?
    The one on this page. It is the schedule Nigeria reports to the World Health Organization, with the most recent record being 2025, and it is what clinics are running now.
    What is the infant vaccine schedule for 2026?
    There is no separate 2026 schedule. The current schedule runs from birth to 15 months for infants, with HPV for girls at nine years, and it changes only when a vaccine is added rather than when the year changes.
    What is the full immunisation schedule for a child?
    Nine visits: at birth, 6 weeks, 10 weeks, 14 weeks, 6 months, 9 months, 12 months, 15 months, and HPV for girls at nine years. The table on this page lists the vaccines given at each one.
    What are the current immunisation programmes in Nigeria?
    Three. Routine immunisation, which is the nine visits given free at government primary health centres. Maternal immunisation, which is tetanus-diphtheria during antenatal care plus vitamin A for children at 6 and 12 months. And periodic mass campaigns for polio and measles-rubella, which add to the routine doses rather than replacing them.
    What is the current immunisation schedule in Nigeria?
    Nine routine visits: at birth, 6, 10 and 14 weeks, 6, 9, 12 and 15 months, and HPV for girls at nine years. The table on this page lists what is given at each one. The vaccines are free at government primary health centres.
    What vaccines does a child get at each age?
    At birth, BCG, oral polio and the hepatitis B birth dose. At 6, 10 and 14 weeks, pentavalent, PCV, polio and rotavirus. At 9 months, measles-rubella, yellow fever and meningitis A. At 15 months, the second measles-rubella. HPV for girls at nine.
    How many injections does a 2 month old get?
    The 6-week visit is three injections plus two doses by mouth. The 10-week visit is two injections plus two by mouth. Nigeria counts the early schedule in weeks, so which one a two month old is due depends on the exact date.
    What are the new vaccines in the schedule?
    Rubella was added to the measles vaccine, so the 9-month and 15-month visits now give measles-rubella rather than measles alone. HPV was added for girls at nine years old.
    What happens if we missed a vaccination?
    You catch up rather than start again. Take the clinic card in and ask, even if the gap is long. The remaining doses are given on a catch-up plan and the protection still builds.
    Are childhood vaccines free in Nigeria?
    The routine vaccines on this schedule are provided free through government primary health centres. Private clinics may charge a consultation or service fee on top.
    Is the yellow fever card the same as the immunisation card?
    No. The child's immunisation card records the routine schedule. The yellow International Certificate of Vaccination is a separate booklet for yellow fever and is what is asked for at borders. Ask for it at the 9-month visit.
