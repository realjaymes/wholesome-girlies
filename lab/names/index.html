Rules for all three

  • The name is saved on her phone only, as one entry (wg_name_v1). It is never sent anywhere, never in an analytics event and never in a link. It follows the same Remember on this device choice as her other entries.
  • First name only, up to 30 characters. It is shown as plain text, never as markup.
  • The backup she saves includes her name, so it comes back when she restores.
  • A shared link and a shared result page never carry her name. Her name is on a result card only if she ticks the box, and the picture is made on her phone.
  • The name is never shown on the quiet pages: the miscarriage guide, warning signs, the Mind Check-in and The Wait Check-in. The greeting stays name-free there.
  • iPhone: the installed app and Safari keep separate storage, so a name typed on the website has to be typed again in the app unless it travels in the backup.

This is a design lab. Nothing here saves anything. The phone mockups use the real site styles. Option A's mockups are what is now live, so the live pages are the reference for it.

Option A: Ask in the app greeting

Tradeoff: the best adoption of the three among people who use the app, because every one of them sees the question once, right at the top. The cost is that we ask before she has a reason to trust us, so some will skip and never see it again. Web-only visitors are never asked, because the question lives on the app home.

iPhone caveat: the installed app and Safari keep separate storage, so a name typed on the website has to be typed again in the app, unless it travels in the backup she saves and restores.

1 Where and when she is asked

Once, on the first open of the app home, under the greeting. Saving hides it. "Not now" hides it for good, and she can still add her name later under Back up or restore.

2 The app home greeting, with her guide

After she saves her name the greeting uses it, and the guide stays beside it. On the first open the four guides stand together, as they do now. On a return visit she has saved tools, so one guide shows.

3 A program home greeting for a buyer

On her Pregnancy home, her name leads the heading and Zainab stays beside it. With no name saved, the heading reads exactly as it does today.

4 A quiz result, and where else her name can show

Her name leads the result headline on her own screen. Putting it on the share card is a tick box that starts off. The Birth Plan Builder already has a name field, so it starts with her saved name.

5 Where she edits or removes her name

Inside Back up or restore, so the one place that holds her phone data also holds her name. Remove clears it from this phone at once.

Option B: Ask at a moment of value

Tradeoff: the fewest prompts, and she has just got something from us when we ask, so the answer rate among people who see it should be good. It also reaches website visitors, not only app users. Fewer people see it at all, because it needs a saved tool or a finished quiz. It sits where the install offer sits, so we would show one of the two per visit.

iPhone caveat: the installed app and Safari keep separate storage, so a name typed on the website has to be typed again in the app, unless it travels in the backup she saves and restores.

1 Where and when she is asked

Nothing on arrival. After her first saved tool entry or first finished quiz, one line sits under the result. Tapping it opens a single field. "Not now" keeps the line away for 14 days, like the install offer.

2 The app home greeting, with her guide

Until she adds a name the app home reads exactly as it does today. After that it greets her by name, with the same guides.

3 A program home greeting for a buyer

A buyer who added her name on a quiz sees it lead the heading on her program home.

4 A quiz result, and where else her name can show

Once she has added it, the result says it. The share card tick box starts off.

5 Where she edits or removes her name

Two doors: Change in the line under any result, and a Your name card in the closing area of the app home.

Option C: Quiet profile only

Tradeoff: no prompt, so no prompt fatigue and nothing that feels pushy. The price is adoption: few people open a small row on purpose, so most greetings would stay name-free. It suits us if we want the feature to exist without asking anyone for anything.

iPhone caveat: the installed app and Safari keep separate storage, so a name typed on the website has to be typed again in the app, unless it travels in the backup she saves and restores.

1 Where and when she is asked

She is never asked. A small Your name row sits in the closing area of the app home and under the program code on program homes. It is the only door.

2 The app home greeting, with her guide

Without a name nothing changes. With one, she is greeted by name every time she opens the app.

3 A program home greeting for a buyer

The row sits under her program code. Once she saves a name the heading uses it.

4 A quiz result, and where else her name can show

Once a name is saved the result says it on her screen, and the share card tick box starts off.

5 Where she edits or removes her name

The same row, opened. It is the same card on the app home and on program homes.
