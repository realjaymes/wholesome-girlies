Red flags in a man: the early signs worth taking seriously

Last updated: September 2026

Written by Cynthia Obinatu, community and editorial lead

A red flag is behaviour that tells you how someone will treat you once they are comfortable. The ones that matter are rarely dramatic. They are small, early and repeated: how he speaks about his last relationship, what he does when you say no, whether he is the same person in front of a waiter as he is in front of you. The dramatic ones arrive later, after you have explained away the small ones for months.

The biggest red flag in a guy

How he responds the first time you disappoint him.

Everything else is downstream of it. Say no to a plan, disagree with him in front of someone, or be unavailable when he wanted you. What happens next tells you more than three months of good behaviour, because good behaviour costs him nothing while he is still winning you.

The early red flags

  • Every ex is crazy. One difficult ex is life. A pattern where no previous woman was reasonable means you are hearing his side of a story he will one day tell about you.
  • He is different with people who cannot do anything for him. Watch him with waiters, drivers, security men and shop staff. That is who he is. You are currently an exception, not the rule.
  • Your no gets negotiated. Not accepted, negotiated. Over something small, like a plan or a photo. Small nos are the test run.
  • He moves very fast. Intense early attention feels like being chosen. It is often a way of skipping the part where you get to assess him.
  • He is vague about the basics. Where he lives, what he does, who he lives with. After weeks of daily talking, vagueness is a decision.
  • He makes you feel like you are too much. One clear question about where things are going should not be treated as a scene.

Red flags that are actually dealbreakers

There is a difference between a warning sign and a stop sign. These are stop signs and none of them improve with love, patience or prayer.

  • Any physical force, including the grabbing that gets called playing.
  • Controlling what you wear, who you see, or whether you work.
  • Reading your phone, tracking your location, or demanding passwords.
  • Threats of any kind, including threats to harm himself if you leave.
  • Contempt. Not anger, contempt: mockery, sneering, being made small in front of others.

If you recognise these, speak to somebody you trust outside the relationship. This is not a page that can help you with that, and there is no version of this that you can fix by being better.

How to tell a red flag from a bad day

Three questions. Has it happened more than once. Does he take responsibility without being pushed. Does anything actually change afterwards.

A bad day is one event, owned, followed by different behaviour. A red flag repeats, gets explained, and stays the same. The apology is not the evidence. The next six weeks are.

The Nigerian version of this

Several behaviours get a cultural pass here that they should not.

A man being sharp with staff or juniors gets read as commanding. Controlling money gets read as providing. Jealousy gets read as caring. And a man who will not be questioned about anything is often described as simply being a man, right up until it is your life.

There is also the specific pressure of being told that a flawed man is better than no man, usually by people who will not be in the marriage. A red flag does not stop being one because you are 31 and your cousin got married last year.

For women in the diaspora, distance hides most of this. You cannot watch how he treats a waiter over a video call. Meet the people around him before you decide anything.

What to do when you see one

Name it to yourself first, plainly, without softening it. Then say it to him once, clearly, and watch the next few weeks rather than the next few hours.

Decide in advance what you would do if it happened again, because deciding while you are attached is close to impossible. That is exactly what our standards and non-negotiables worksheet is for. If you want to check what you are seeing against both lists, use the green and red flags checker.

Common questions

What is the biggest red flag in a guy?
How he responds the first time you disappoint him. Saying no to a plan, disagreeing with him publicly, or being unavailable will tell you more than months of good behaviour, because good behaviour costs nothing while he is still winning you.
What are common red flags in a person?
Speaking badly about every past partner, treating people who cannot help him differently, negotiating rather than accepting a no, moving very fast, being vague about basic facts, and making you feel your reasonable questions are too much.
What are the red flags that are dealbreakers?
Any physical force, controlling what you wear or who you see, reading your phone or tracking you, threats of any kind including self-harm threats, and contempt. None of these improve over time.
How do I tell a red flag from a bad day?
Ask whether it has happened more than once, whether he takes responsibility without being pushed, and whether anything changes afterwards. The apology is not the evidence, the next six weeks are.
What are 10 red flags in a relationship?
Every ex being crazy, treating service staff badly, negotiating your no, moving too fast, vagueness about basics, making you feel like too much, controlling money, jealousy framed as care, refusing to be questioned, and contempt.
