Situationship: what it means, and how to know you are in one

Last updated: September 2026

Written by Cynthia Obinatu, community and editorial lead

A situationship is a relationship in everything but name. You behave like a couple, you may be intimate, you talk most days, and neither of you has agreed what it is. It is not the same as casual dating, because in casual dating both people know the terms. In a situationship the terms are deliberately left unsaid, and that usually suits one person more than the other.

What exactly is a situationship?

Two things have to be true at once. You are getting most of what a relationship gives, and you have no claim on any of it. That combination is the whole definition.

The word entered dictionaries only recently, which tells you it describes something people needed a name for. What it names is the gap between how something feels and what it has been agreed to be.

How do I know if I am in a situationship?

  • It has been going on for months and neither of you has defined it.
  • You are intimate, but you are not introduced.
  • Plans are always short notice, and you are rarely part of anything scheduled.
  • You do not know whether you are the only one, and asking feels like it would break something.
  • You have had the conversation before and nothing changed after it.
  • You feel more anxious after seeing him than before.

The last one carries the most information. A real relationship in its early weeks is uncertain but it settles you. A situationship does the opposite, and it does it repeatedly. If you want a straight answer on where you stand, our situationship checker walks you through it.

Is a situationship the same as dating?

No. Dating, even casually, has an agreed shape. Both people know whether it is exclusive, both know roughly where it is going, and either can refer to it without a disclaimer.

A situationship has none of that, and the absence is not an accident. Someone is getting the benefits of a relationship without agreeing to its cost, and the reason it is never defined is that defining it would end the arrangement.

How long do situationships last?

Far longer than people expect, because nothing inside one creates pressure to end it. Months is normal, a year or more is common, and they usually end for an external reason rather than a decision: one person moves, meets someone else, or finally runs out of patience.

That is the trap. A bad relationship ends because it is bad. A situationship is comfortable enough not to end and unsatisfying enough to cost you years.

Is a situationship good or bad?

It depends entirely on whether both of you want the same thing from it, and that is rarer than it sounds.

If you both genuinely want something undefined and low-commitment right now, it is not a problem, it is an arrangement. The honest test is simple: if he said tomorrow that he wants it to stay exactly as it is forever, would you be relieved or would something drop in your chest? If it is the second, this is costing you, and the cost is not visible month to month. It is visible in years.

Is a situationship real love?

The feelings are usually real. That is what makes it hard, and it is why being told to just leave is useless advice.

But love that will not be named in front of other people is not doing the job love is meant to do. Commitment is not a feeling, it is a decision someone makes out loud and can be held to. You can genuinely love someone who will not make it, and leaving is still the right call.

Red flags in a situationship

  • He changes the subject every time the future comes up.
  • You are a secret from his family, his friends, or the internet.
  • He reappears exactly when you start pulling away.
  • Your feelings are treated as an overreaction whenever you raise them.
  • He says he is not ready, and continues to take everything a relationship gives.
  • You have started editing yourself so he does not think you want too much.

That last one is the real damage. Our guide to the green flags that matter covers the opposite side of this.

The Nigerian version of this

Two local pressures make situationships harder to leave here.

The first is the age conversation. When family, church and friends are all counting, a woman in her late twenties or thirties is told, directly and indirectly, that something undefined is better than nothing. That is what keeps a situationship running for two years. It is not weakness, it is arithmetic being done out loud by people who are not living it.

The second is that being introduced means something specific here. In Nigerian families, meeting people is not a casual step, it is close to a declaration. That gives a man an easy and respectable-sounding reason to delay for months, and it gives you a genuinely harder time reading the signal. The useful question is not whether you have met his family. It is whether anyone in his life knows your name.

For women abroad, distance supplies the same excuse indefinitely.

How to get out of one

One conversation, with a decision attached before you start it. Say what you want. Ask what he wants. Decide in advance what you will do if the answer is not it, and then do that.

The conversation is not the hard part. The hard part is that you have almost certainly had it before and accepted an answer that was not an answer. I am not ready yet is not an answer. Let us see how it goes is not an answer. Our Talk Planner is built to stop that happening, and the non-negotiables worksheet is where you decide what you will not accept before you are in the room.

Common questions

What does it mean to be in a situationship?
It means you have a relationship in everything but name: the behaviour, often the intimacy, and the daily contact, with no agreement about what it is and no claim on any of it.
How do I know if I am in a situationship?
It has run for months undefined, you are intimate but not introduced, plans are always last minute, you do not know if you are the only one, and you feel more anxious after seeing him rather than settled.
Is a situationship like dating?
No. Casual dating has agreed terms both people understand. A situationship leaves the terms unsaid, and that absence usually benefits one person more than the other.
How long do situationships last?
Often months and sometimes years, because nothing inside one creates pressure to end it. They usually end for an outside reason rather than a decision.
Is a situationship good or bad?
It is fine if both people genuinely want the same undefined thing. If the idea of it staying exactly as it is forever makes your stomach drop, it is costing you.
Is a situationship real love?
The feelings are usually real. But love that will not be named in front of anyone else is not doing what commitment does, and you can love someone who will not commit and still be right to leave.
What are the red flags in a situationship?
Avoiding the future, keeping you a secret, reappearing when you pull away, treating your feelings as an overreaction, saying he is not ready while taking everything a relationship gives, and you editing yourself so you do not seem to want too much.
