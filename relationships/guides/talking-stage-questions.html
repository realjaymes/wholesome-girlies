Talking stage questions: what to ask, and what the answers tell you

Last updated: October 2026

Written by Cynthia Obinatu, community and editorial lead

Talking-stage questions help you find out early whether you want the same life. The information is cheap to get now, and you are not yet too attached to act on it. The questions below are grouped by what they reveal, and many come with what a good answer and a bad answer sound like.

How do I ask without it becoming an interview?

Ask two or three of these per conversation. Ask them the way you would ask a friend, and answer them yourself too. If a reasonable question makes him defensive, you have learned something more useful than the answer would have been.

Listen for specifics. Vague answers to specific questions are themselves the answer, especially after weeks of daily talking.

What are 10 good questions to ask during the talking stage?

These ten cover what matters most. Ask two or three at a time, over several nights.

  • What are you looking for right now?
  • What does the next two years look like for you?
  • When last were you in a relationship, and what ended it?
  • Are you talking to anyone else?
  • Do you want children, and roughly when?
  • How close are you to your family, and how much do they decide?
  • What does your faith look like day to day?
  • How do you handle money, and who do you support?
  • What does an argument look like for you?
  • What is something about you that people find difficult?

If you want twenty questions to ask someone, the sections below add more on each topic, plus a short list of deeper ones. Nobody needs to ask all twenty. The test is still whether a particular answer would change what you do next.

Questions about where this is going

  • What are you looking for right now? A good answer is specific and mentions a timeline. A bad one is I am just going with the flow, repeated for three months.
  • When last were you in a relationship, and what ended it? Listen for whether he can name a single thing that was his fault.
  • What does the next two years look like for you? Listen for marriage, city, work and children. If you are not in the picture by month three, notice it.
  • Are you talking to anyone else? You are allowed to ask this. His reaction to being asked is half the information.

Questions about money

Money is the topic people avoid most, and it causes real trouble in marriages. Ask how he thinks about it, and leave his salary out of it.

  • How do you feel about a wife who earns more than you? Watch his face as well as his words.
  • Who do you support financially, and is that ongoing? Black tax is real, and supporting family is fine. Not knowing about it is the problem.
  • Do you save, and what for? How he answers tells you whether there is a plan or a vibe.
  • How would we handle money if we married, separate or joint? There is no right answer. What matters is whether he has thought about it.

Questions about family and faith

  • How close are you to your family, and how much do they decide? In Nigerian marriages this is rarely a small question.
  • What does your faith look like day to day? Ask what he actually does, beyond naming a denomination.
  • Would your family have a problem with where I am from? Better asked in month two than year two.
  • What did your parents' marriage teach you? Most people either copy it or react against it, and both are worth knowing.

Questions about children

  • Do you want children, and roughly when? The when matters most.
  • What would you expect to change about my work if we had a baby? This one question resolves more than an hour of general talk.
  • Who did the caring in your house growing up? It is usually the template he is working from without realising.

Questions about conflict

Look at how he behaves when things do not go his way. It predicts more than most other things you can learn.

  • What does an argument look like for you? He may shout, go silent, walk out, or talk it through.
  • When did you last apologise to someone and mean it? A man who cannot produce an example is telling you one.
  • What do you do when you are stressed? Then notice whether it matches what you have seen.

What are 10 deep questions to ask someone?

Deep questions ask for a story rather than a yes or no, and they work best once you have been talking for a few weeks.

  • What has been the hardest year of your life, and what did you learn from it?
  • What do you want your life to look like in ten years?
  • What is something you used to believe about love that you no longer do?
  • What do you need from a partner when you are struggling?
  • What are you most afraid of getting wrong in a relationship?
  • Who has shaped how you see marriage, and how?
  • When have you changed your mind about something important?
  • What does being loved well look like to you?
  • What is something you are still working on about yourself?
  • What would make you walk away from a relationship?

Answer them yourself as well. Questions that only go one way start to feel like an interview.

Which three questions save me a year?

If you only ask three, ask these.

  • What are we? Ask it once, plainly, around week eight to twelve.
  • What would have to be true for you to be ready? This shows whether not ready means anything.
  • What is something about you that people find difficult? One answer shows whether he is self-aware.

What are flirty questions to ask a guy, and what makes a boy blush?

Questions make a guy blush when they are personal, playful and about the two of you. They are for fun, and they do not assess compatibility. Use these twenty-one when the mood is light.

  • What was your first impression of me?
  • What is the first thing you noticed about me?
  • What is the best compliment you have ever received?
  • What would our perfect first date look like to you?
  • What do you find most attractive in a person, apart from looks?
  • What song makes you think of me?
  • What is the most romantic thing you have ever done?
  • What is your favourite thing about the way we talk?
  • If we were stuck together for a weekend, what would we do?
  • What makes you smile when you read my messages?
  • What is something small that makes you feel special?
  • Do you remember what you were thinking on our first date?
  • What do you think I am like when I am nervous?
  • What is one thing about me you want to know more about?
  • What is a little habit of yours that most people never see?
  • What are you like when you have a crush on someone?
  • What is your idea of a perfect Sunday together?
  • What would you cook for me?
  • What would you want me to say to you right now?
  • What is the best date you have ever been on?
  • What would make you text me first?

Mix one or two of these in with the real questions above, and notice whether he asks any back.

Which questions are not worth asking?

Most lists online are flirty questions that keep a conversation going. They are fine as entertainment, and they tell you nothing. Would you rather, what is your love language, and twenty-one questions to make him blush do not assess anything.

The test for a real question is whether a particular answer would change what you do next. If no answer would change anything, it is small talk.

What do I do with his answers?

Write them down, so that in month five you remember what he said in month one. Memory rewrites itself once you are attached.

Then check them against what you decided you would not accept. Our standards and non-negotiables worksheet is where that list lives, and how to set yours explains how to write ones that hold up. When it is time for the conversation itself, the Talk Planner walks you through it.

Common questions

What to ask during the talking stage?
Ask what he is looking for and by when, what ended his last relationship, how he thinks about money, what his family expects, whether and when he wants children, and what he does in an argument. Two or three per conversation is enough.
What are 10 good questions to ask?
What are you looking for right now, what ended your last relationship, what do the next two years look like, are you talking to anyone else, how do you feel about a wife who earns more, who do you support financially, how much do your family decide, do you want children and when, what does an argument look like for you, and what do people find difficult about you.
What are good questions to ask a guy in the talking stage?
The ones where a particular answer would change what you do next. If no answer would change anything, it is small talk rather than a question.
How many questions should I ask?
Ask two or three per conversation, spread over weeks, and answer them yourself too. A list fired off at once turns into an interview and tells you less.
Are flirty questions useful in the talking stage?
They are entertainment. Questions designed to make someone blush keep a conversation going but tell you nothing about whether you want the same life.
What are 10 deep questions to ask someone?
Deep questions ask for a story rather than a yes or no. Good examples are what the hardest year of your life taught you, what you need from a partner when you are struggling, and what would make you walk away from a relationship.
What are 21 flirty questions to ask a guy?
Flirty questions are personal and playful, such as what his first impression of you was, what the perfect first date would be, and what song reminds him of you. They are good for fun, but they are not a way to assess compatibility.
What questions make a boy blush?
Questions about the two of you, such as his first impression of you or what makes him smile when he reads your messages. Keep them light and mix them in with real questions.
What are 20 questions to ask someone?
Pick from the guide's ten core questions plus the deep and flirty lists. Ask two or three a conversation and spread them over weeks.
