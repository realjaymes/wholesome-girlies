Omugwo: Your Mum or His Mum?

Your mum offers to come. His mum offers to come. Both of them mean well. Pick what you would do in nine omugwo dilemmas and find out which team you are on.

1. The baby is due soon. Who do you want for omugwo first?
2. How long should omugwo run?
3. Who runs the kitchen?
4. Who sets the house rules for the month?
5. Who handles the visitors?
6. Your mum's visa is taking long and the baby is nearly here. What do you do?
7. Who sits up at night with the baby so you can rest?
8. The two families disagree about a small thing. What do you do?
9. Omugwo is over. How do you thank the mothers?

One thing to settle in each dilemma

    These are made-up dilemmas. They are for fun and conversation, and they say nothing about your family. Real families have more history than four answers can hold. This is educational, not medical advice, and it is sourced but not yet reviewed by a clinician. Your picks stay on your phone. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.

    If you are struggling after birth

    Many mums feel low or overwhelmed after a baby, and you do not have to carry it alone. Talk to your doctor, your midwife or a counsellor. In Nigeria you can call the Mentally Aware Nigeria Initiative (MANI) on 0809 111 6264. If you ever have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, treat it as an emergency and get help immediately. Our Mind Check-in is a place to start.

    When to see a doctor

    Omugwo is care. It does not replace your postpartum checks at the clinic. Do not skip an appointment or wait out something that worries you because your mother is in the house. Our postpartum warning signs guide sets out what needs a doctor the same day.

    Written by Cynthia Obinatu, community and editorial lead

    How the Omugwo: Your Mum or His Mum? quiz works

    You will see nine omugwo dilemmas that Nigerian families know well, from who comes first to who runs the kitchen. Pick the answer closest to what you would do. Your picks give you a team. There are no votes to count here, so choose for yourself.

    1. Read one situation at a time.
    2. Pick the answer closest to you.
    3. See your result.
    4. Send it to the group chat.

    What your result means

    Your result shows the team that appears most in your answers. Every team here respects both families. Omugwo is a gift of time and care from a mother or mother-in-law, and no team is better than the others. The right answer is the one you and your husband agree on, and the one that both mothers hear from you early and with love.

    • Team My Mum. You feel most at ease with your own mother, and that matters in the weeks after birth. Tell your husband and his mother early and warmly, so his family feels included, and find a part of the month that is theirs.
    • Team His Mum. You want to honour his family and follow the elder in the house. That can build a strong bond with his mother. Say what you need out loud too, because she can only help with what she knows.
    • Team Both. You want both families in. That takes planning: dates, roles and one person who explains the plan to everyone. If you agree it before the baby arrives, both mothers will feel valued.
    • Team Just Us, thank you. You want to find your feet as a family first, with visits instead of a long stay. You are choosing a quiet start, and that rejects nobody. Tell both mothers early what you would love from them, so they can still be part of it.

    For the full story of omugwo, read our omugwo guide. To share the load at home, try the Partner Support Planner.

    Common questions

    Is one team better than the others?
    No. Each team respects both families. The best plan is the one you and your husband agree on and share with both mothers early.
    What if my mother-in-law and my mum both want to come?
    Many families have this. Agree the dates and roles with your husband first, then tell both mothers together. Our omugwo guide has more on this.
    What if neither mother can come?
    You are still a good mother. Plan helpers, meals and video calls ahead, and ask your husband to share the nights. Our Partner Support Planner can help.
    Does omugwo replace my postpartum checks?
    No. Omugwo is care and it does not replace your checks at the clinic. Keep every appointment, even with your mother in the house.
    Is this private?
    Yes. Your picks stay on your phone. If you share your result, the link and the picture show your result only, never your picks.
