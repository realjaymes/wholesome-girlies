Guide

What causes low sperm count, and what a couple can do

Last updated: November 2026

Written by Cynthia Obinatu, community and editorial lead, and James Praise, founder and researcher · Checked against published guidance from MedlinePlus, National Health Service, PubMed Central and World Health Organization. Not yet reviewed by a clinician, so read it with our medical policy.

Low sperm count can come from an infection in the tubes that make and carry sperm, a swollen vein in the scrotum (the skin bag that holds the testicles) called a varicocele, a hormone problem, testicles that did not come down properly in childhood, some medicines, anabolic steroids, smoking, heavy drinking and heat around the testicles. A semen analysis in a laboratory is the test that measures his count. A low result does not always mean a couple cannot conceive. The National Health Service (NHS) says it may still be possible to get pregnant naturally.

The World Health Organization (WHO) says infertility can come from either partner. It also says women are often assumed to be the infertile one, whether they are or not. A semen analysis early on is the fair place to start.

What this covers. This guide explains the causes, the test and the questions couples ask. It does not cover prescription treatment. That is a doctor's decision after tests. This guide has not been reviewed by a clinician.

What are signs of male infertility?

The clearest sign is that a couple has been trying for a long time without a pregnancy. The WHO counts it as infertility after 12 months or more of regular sex without contraception. A man can feel completely well and still have a low count. The laboratory result is what tells you.

Some of the causes leave a history you can ask about. Has he had mumps that affected his testicles, an injury or surgery there, or a sexually transmitted infection? Were his testicles brought down by surgery when he was a child? Does he use steroids at the gym? Each of these is on the list of causes below, and each is worth telling the doctor.

What causes low sperm count?

The causes fall into a few groups.

  • Infection. The WHO lists infection of the genital tract among the male causes. MedlinePlus, from the United States National Library of Medicine, names infections and mumps affecting the testicles. A review in PubMed Central describes how gonorrhoea and chlamydia can scar the tubes that carry sperm.
  • Varicocele. This is a swollen vein in the scrotum, a bit like a swollen vein in the leg. The same review estimates it affects 15 to 20% of men in general, and 35 to 40% of men who have never fathered a child and are struggling to.
  • Testicles that did not come down. The NHS lists undescended testicles as a cause.
  • Hormones and health conditions. The WHO names hormone problems, and MedlinePlus lists diabetes that is not controlled.
  • Medicines and drugs. The WHO names anabolic steroids and chemotherapy. The NHS says some medicines, and illegal drugs such as marijuana and cocaine, can affect semen quality. Never stop a medicine your doctor gave you without asking first.
  • Habits and weight. The WHO lists smoking, heavy drinking and obesity as factors that can affect fertility.
  • Heat. The NHS notes a link between a hotter scrotum and poorer semen quality. It also says it is not certain that loose underwear improves fertility.

Is infection a common cause in Nigeria?

The Nigerian figures suggest it is worth checking. A study at Central Hospital, Benin City, looked at 355 men whose partners had come for infertility care. Two out of every three men (66.5%) had an abnormal semen result and 22.8% had a low sperm count. Almost half (43.6%) had bacteria growing in the semen sample. Those men were more than four times as likely to have an abnormal result.

A study of 225 men at Ahmadu Bello University Teaching Hospital, Zaria, found that the worse the varicocele, the lower the sperm count tended to be. Both studies come from hospital clinics. They describe men who were already having trouble conceiving, and they say little about Nigerian men in general.

What are signs of unhealthy sperm?

You cannot see unhealthy sperm. The NHS describes three problems a laboratory looks for: too few sperm, sperm that do not move properly, and sperm with an abnormal shape. The result sheet shows a number for each. "Motility" on the sheet means how well the sperm move, and "morphology" means their shape.

The WHO's 2021 laboratory manual gives lower reference limits, summarised in a paper in PubMed Central: 16 million sperm per millilitre, 39 million sperm in the whole sample, 42% of sperm moving, and 1.4 millilitres of semen. The same paper warns that these numbers do not cleanly separate fertile men from men who will struggle. A man just under a limit may still father a child. Take the sheet to a doctor and read it together.

How can I check my low sperm count at home?

Home sperm test kits exist, but they do not replace a laboratory test. A review in PubMed Central found home devices cannot be treated as a full replacement for a standard semen analysis and should not be the only test. MedlinePlus says a home test gives less information than a laboratory one.

Ask your doctor or fertility clinic which laboratory they trust for a semen analysis. Then ask the laboratory how many days he should go without ejaculating before the sample, whether he can collect it at home, and how quickly it must reach them. MedlinePlus says a man may need two or more samples a week or two apart, because the count changes from day to day. One low result is a reason to repeat the test.

Can you still get pregnant with a low sperm count?

It may still be possible, says the NHS. The NHS suggests having sex every 2 to 3 days without contraception, so you do not have to guess the fertile days. The NHS also says some people get pregnant quickly and for others it takes longer.

What matters is how low the count is, how well the sperm move, the woman's own fertility and how long you have been trying. A doctor who sees both of your results can tell you more than any number on its own. Our fertile window guide and the ovulation calculator show which days in her cycle count most.

How can I increase my sperm count?

There is no quick way. Sperm take about 74 days to develop, according to a paper in PubMed Central. A change he makes today may take two to three months to show on a test.

The changes with support behind them are the ones the WHO and NHS name: stop smoking, drink less (the NHS advises no more than 14 units of alcohol a week), stop anabolic steroids and recreational drugs, keep to a healthy weight, and keep the testicles away from long periods of heat. A man who has had a sexually transmitted infection, or signs of one, should get tested.

Supplements have less evidence behind them than most adverts suggest. Our guide on male fertility supplements covers what the large trials found, and why the herbal manpower products sold in markets need care.

How do you fix low sperm count?

It depends on the cause. That is why the test and a doctor's visit come first. Some causes can be dealt with once they are found, and fertility treatment exists for couples where the count stays low. A fertility doctor or a urologist (a doctor for the urinary and male reproductive organs) decides which option fits, after the tests. A pharmacy counter or a herbal seller cannot make that decision.

What if he does not want to be tested?

Many Nigerian men feel a low count is an attack on their manhood. In a study of infertile men in Osogbo, published in Cureus in 2025, men spoke plainly about how the blame shifts once a man is tested.

The test is one sample and one result sheet. If you need help raising it without a fight, the his fertility checklist walks through the conversation, and the fertility appointment questions help you both plan what to ask the doctor.

When to see a doctor

See a doctor together after 12 months of trying without a pregnancy. That is how the WHO defines infertility. The NHS says go sooner if the woman is 36 or older, or if either of you already knows of a fertility problem.

He should not wait a year if he has had mumps affecting the testicles, an injury or surgery there, or a sexually transmitted infection. Those are known causes, and the doctor will want to know. Tell the doctor everything he takes, including herbal products and gym supplements.

Common questions

Can low sperm motility be cured?
It depends on the cause, and nobody can promise a result. Motility means how well the sperm move. A doctor looks for the cause first and explains the options after the tests.
What causes low sperm count in young men?
The same things as in older men: infection, a varicocele, testicles that did not come down, hormone problems, anabolic steroids, smoking, heavy drinking, drugs and heat. The WHO names anabolic steroids directly, and young men at the gym are the ones most likely to use them.
How long does it take to improve sperm count?
Sperm take about 74 days to develop. Wait two to three months after a change before repeating the test.
Is a home sperm test enough?
No. Home kits give less information than a laboratory semen analysis, and a review of the devices says they should not be the only test.
Is one low result final?
No. MedlinePlus says the count changes from day to day, and a man may need two or more samples a week or two apart.
