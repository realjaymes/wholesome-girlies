Period Tracker & Next Period Calculator

Enter the first day of your last period and your usual cycle length, and we will estimate your next three periods. Over time, tracking helps you see your own pattern.

Your next three periods (estimated)

Educational, not medical advice. These are estimates based on a regular cycle. Real cycles vary from month to month, so treat this as a guide. It runs in your browser and stores nothing.

When to see a doctor

See a doctor for periods that suddenly become very irregular, very heavy, very painful, or that stop for three months or more when you are not pregnant. These are worth checking, not worrying about alone.

How this calculator works

It does arithmetic, and it is worth knowing exactly which arithmetic, because that tells you where it can be wrong. You give it two things: the first day of your last period, and your usual cycle length. It adds your cycle length to that date to get the next first day, then repeats twice more. That is the entire method.

It does not measure anything about your body. It has no way of knowing that you travelled, fell ill, started a new job, lost weight or came off the pill, and every one of those can move a cycle. Nothing is sent anywhere either. The dates stay in your browser on this device.

If you are registering for antenatal care at a clinic in Nigeria, the first question you will be asked is the first day of your last period, because the due date is counted from it. Having the date written down instead of guessed is the single most useful thing this tool gives you.

What your result means, and what it does not

Read each date as the middle of a window, not as an appointment. If your own cycles vary by three or four days, which is ordinary, your period can arrive three or four days either side of the date shown and nothing is wrong. The estimate gets better as you feed it a truer average, which is why tracking three or four months beats guessing once.

Two things the result is not. It is not a pregnancy test, so a late date on the screen tells you nothing that a test cannot tell you properly. It is not birth control either, because the fertile days it implies are an estimate of an estimate.

Most of us grew up hearing that a cycle is 28 days, repeated in school and by older women at home as though it were a rule. It is an average, not a rule. If yours runs 31 or 33 days and runs there consistently, that is your normal, and calculating against 28 will make you feel late every month for no reason.

How do I calculate my next period?

Count from the first day of your last period, not the day it ended. That first day is day one. Add your usual cycle length, and the day you land on is day one of your next period. If your last period started on 3 September and your cycle is 30 days, your next one is due on 3 October.

This is where most people go wrong, because the bleeding ends somewhere around day five and it feels like the natural place to start counting. It is not. Every calculation on this page, and in every app you will use, starts at the first day of bleeding.

Your cycle length is the gap between one first day and the next. Count it over three or four months and take the average, because no two months are identical. Around 28 days is common, and anything from 21 to 35 days is normal according to the National Health Service. A 30-day cycle is not a long cycle. It is a 30-day cycle.

How do I calculate if my period is late?

Late means past the day you expected it, counted the way above. That only means something if you know your own average, which is the real reason to track. A woman who knows she runs 31 days can tell on day 34 that something is different. A woman who assumes 28 because that is the number everybody repeats will think she is six days late every single month.

A few days either side is ordinary. Illness, travel, a hard month at work, weight change and stress all move it. If your period has not come and there is any chance you are pregnant, take a test rather than counting again. Tests are sold over the counter in pharmacies here and do not need a prescription.

Can I get pregnant 1, 2 or 3 days after my period?

Yes. It is less likely than mid-cycle, but it is possible, and treating the days after your period as safe is how people get surprised. Sperm can survive for several days inside the body, so sex on the last day of bleeding can still meet an egg released later. The shorter your cycle, the sooner you ovulate, and the smaller that gap becomes.

Read this page as a guide to your own pattern, not as contraception. The ovulation calculator estimates your fertile window, and the fertile window guide explains what it is actually estimating.

Are period tracker apps accurate for ovulation?

They are good at predicting your next period and much weaker at predicting ovulation, and the difference matters if you are trying to conceive. A calendar predicts. It does not detect. It is working from the pattern you typed in, so it cannot see the month your body does something else.

An analysis of 7,043 women's tracked cycles, published in the Journal of Medical Internet Research, found that ovulation does not sit reliably in the middle of the cycle. Most of the variation lives in the first half, before ovulation, while the second half is steadier. That is why assuming day 14 performs poorly for most women. A separate review in Frontiers in Public Health looked at apps advertising help with conception and found most gave no evidence for the method behind their predictions.

So use the date as a starting point and watch your own body alongside it. If you are trying to conceive and the months are passing, ovulation test strips or a scan at a clinic will tell you far more than any calendar can.

Can an app tell me if I am pregnant?

No. No app can, including the popular ones. An app knows the dates you gave it. It can tell you your period is late, which you can also see, and it can guess. Only a pregnancy test detects the hormone that confirms it. If your test is negative and your period still has not come after a week, take another one, and see a doctor if it stays that way.

When your cycle is worth taking to a doctor

The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists describes the menstrual cycle as a vital sign, in the same category as blood pressure. A cycle that changes character is information about your health, not an inconvenience to manage.

Worth a doctor's time: periods that suddenly become much heavier or much more painful than your normal, bleeding between periods, cycles that swing wildly from month to month, cycles consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35, or no period for three months when you are not pregnant. Take your tracked dates with you. A doctor can do far more with four months of real dates than with "it comes when it wants".

Common questions

Do I count 28 days from the start or the end of my period?
From the start. Day one is the first day of bleeding, not the day it stops. Add your cycle length to that date to get the first day of your next period.
How do I calculate a 30-day menstrual cycle?
Take the first day of your last period and add 30 days. That is day one of the next one. Your fertile window sits roughly in the middle, but it moves, so do not treat it as fixed.
How do I work out my last period date if I did not write it down?
Work backwards from anything you do remember, a trip, a payday, an event. Then start recording the first day from now on. Three or four months of dates gives you your real average, which is worth more than any estimate.
Can I get pregnant right after my period?
Yes, though it is less likely than mid-cycle. Sperm survive several days, so sex near the end of bleeding can still result in pregnancy, especially on a short cycle. Do not use the calendar as contraception.
Are period tracker apps accurate?
They predict periods reasonably well and ovulation much less well. A calendar works from the pattern you entered, so it cannot see a month when your body behaves differently.
Can Flo or any app tell me if I am pregnant?
No. An app only knows the dates you typed in. It can flag that your period is late. Only a pregnancy test can confirm a pregnancy.
Is a 30 or 32 day cycle normal?
Yes. Anything from 21 to 35 days is within the normal range. The number that matters is whether yours is consistent for you, not whether it equals 28.
