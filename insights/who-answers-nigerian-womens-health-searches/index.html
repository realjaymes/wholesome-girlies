Who answers Nigerian women's health searches

We checked the first ten organic Google results for 237 searches made from Nigeria. Nigerian sources held 45 of the 2,370 places. Most of the advice a woman sees comes from somewhere else.

Published 26 November 2026

By James Praise, founder and researcher
237searches studied
2,370organic result slots
572distinct domains
1.9%of slots from Nigerian sources

A Nigerian woman searching for an ovulation date, a pregnancy symptom or help with a newborn is usually given an answer written for another health system. The National Health Service (NHS) alone appeared 91 times. Mayo Clinic appeared 74 times and Cleveland Clinic 71.

Tommy's appeared 68 times, Healthline 57 and American Pregnancy Association 54.

Five findings

Nigerian sources held 45 of 2,370 result slots

That is 1.9% of the sample. A woman can search from Nigeria and read an entire first page without seeing one Nigerian hospital, public body, publisher or company.

Foreign health publishers held 799 slots

They took 33.7% of all the results we checked. Their advice can be useful, but the next step may assume access to the NHS or a United States insurance plan. It may also name a medicine sold abroad or a hospital process that does not match Nigeria.

Relationships had no Nigerian source in 200 results

We checked 20 searches about talking stages, situationships, red flags, non-negotiables and communication. None of their top-ten results came from a Nigerian-owned source or Nigerian public institution.

Pregnancy answers were 39.6% foreign health publishers

Foreign health publishers held 368 of 930 pregnancy slots.

Nigerian sources held 21, or 2.3%. This matters when a woman needs to know what antenatal care costs, where a scan fits or how a hospital handles labour.

72 of 79 sampled topics had no Nigerian source

That is 91.1% of the in-scope topic clusters with at least one sampled search. The missing topics included ovulation calculators, period calculators, signs of labour, pregnancy tests at home, colic, newborn sleep position, postpartum psychosis and talking-stage questions.

Method

In September 2026, we searched Google in English from the Nigeria location for 237 questions about fertility, pregnancy, postpartum, parenting and relationships. We counted the first ten organic results for each search. We called a source Nigerian only when it belonged to a Nigerian publisher, company, government body or university.

Limits

  • This is a September 2026 snapshot. Google results change.
  • The sample is limited to English searches from the Nigeria location. Another language or city may return different pages.
  • The study measures visibility. We did not assess the medical quality of each page.
  • Ownership can be hard to verify for private companies. We used public company information available when the study was prepared.
  • Each sampled search counts once in this analysis.
